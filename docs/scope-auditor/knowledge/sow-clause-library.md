# SOW clause library

Reference for the Scope Creep Auditor. Plain-English clauses that close the gaps in the
taxonomy.

**These are drafting starting points, not legal advice.** Always tell the user to have
their contract reviewed properly. Always rewrite a clause to name the client's actual
deliverables, numbers, and dates before presenting it — a clause naming "the four blog
posts and twelve social assets" is worth far more than one naming "the Deliverables".

---

## 1. Revision cap
*Closes A2.*

> This engagement includes [two] rounds of revisions on each deliverable. A round means
> one consolidated set of feedback covering the whole deliverable. Additional rounds, or
> feedback arriving after a round has been actioned, are billed at [£X]/hour in [30]-minute
> increments, quoted and approved in writing before work begins.

Consolidation matters as much as the number. Two rounds of scattered daily notes cost
more than four consolidated ones.

## 2. Deliverable schedule
*Closes A1, A3.*

> The following, and only the following, are included:
> [exact list, with quantity, format, dimensions, and word count for each]
> Anything not on this list is out of scope and quoted separately.

"And only the following" is the load-bearing phrase.

## 3. Single approver
*Closes C1, C2.*

> [Name] is the sole approver for this engagement. Feedback from other parties is welcome
> but must be consolidated by [Name] into a single response per round. We act on
> [Name]'s instruction only. If the approver changes, we will review the remaining scope
> together before continuing.

The last sentence is what protects you from C2.

## 4. Client dependencies
*Closes B3, D1.*

> Delivery dates assume we receive [list: copy, brand assets, CMS access, interviews] by
> [date]. Each working day of delay in client-supplied material or approval moves all
> subsequent dates by one working day. Deadlines are not compressed to absorb delay.

One-for-one shifting is the fair version and clients accept it readily.

## 5. Acceptance and deemed approval
*Closes C3. The clause that gets invoices paid.*

> A deliverable is accepted when approved in writing, or [five] working days after
> submission if no written feedback has been received. Accepted deliverables are final and
> further changes are handled as new work.

Without a deemed-approval window, a silent client can keep a project open forever.

## 6. Change order threshold
*Closes D6.*

> Requests outside the deliverable schedule are handled by change order. Anything
> estimated under [30] minutes we will absorb at our discretion. Anything above is quoted
> and confirmed in writing before we start.

The absorbed allowance is what keeps this from feeling petty. Keep it.

## 7. Meeting allowance
*Closes B1.*

> The fee includes [six] hours of meetings, including calls, workshops, and reviews.
> Additional meeting time is billed at [£X]/hour. Written updates are unlimited and free.

Offering unlimited written updates makes the cap read as generous rather than defensive.

## 8. Out-of-scope rate card
*Closes A4, D5, D6.*

> Out-of-scope work is billed at: strategy [£X]/hr · copy [£X]/hr · design [£X]/hr ·
> production [£X]/hr. Rates hold for the duration of this engagement.

A published rate makes extra requests a decision instead of an argument.

## 9. Source material and access
*Closes D1.*

> The client supplies: [list]. Where the client cannot supply an item, we can source it
> at [£X]/hr plus third-party costs, agreed in advance. Work pauses on any deliverable
> blocked by missing material.

## 10. Rights, source files, and reuse
*Closes D3.*

> On final payment, ownership of the delivered final files transfers to the client for
> use in [defined channels/territories/term]. Working files, project files, and unused
> concepts remain ours and can be released for [£X]. Third-party licences (fonts, stock,
> music) are the client's cost and are listed at handover.

"On final payment" is the leverage. Do not give it away.

## 11. Post-delivery support window
*Closes B2.*

> The fee includes [10] working days of support after final delivery, covering fixes to
> delivered work. Training, new requests, and optimisation after that window are quoted
> separately.

Distinguish fixing your work (yours) from changing your work (theirs).

## 12. Scope freeze
*Closes A4, C2.*

> Scope is frozen at [date / milestone]. Changes after that point are handled by change
> order and may affect the delivery date.

## 13. Discovery as a paid phase
*Closes B4, D2.*

> Discovery is a separate priced phase of [£X], delivering [named artefact]. The
> execution phase is quoted on completion of discovery.

Selling discovery separately is the single highest-leverage change a freelancer can make
to an ambiguous brief. It converts a risky fixed-price project into two bounded ones, and
gives an honest exit if the project turns out to be something else.

## 14. Kill fee
> If the engagement is cancelled after work has begun, completed and in-progress
> deliverables are invoiced pro rata, with a minimum of [25]% of the total fee.

## 15. Late payment pause
> Work pauses on any invoice more than [14] days overdue. Timelines shift by the length
> of the pause.

---

## Selection guidance

Never present more than six clauses. A reply carrying fifteen clauses reads as distrust
and loses deals. Rank by the score rubric: the clause closing the highest-scoring finding
goes first, and stop when the remaining findings are worth less than the goodwill.

For a brief scoring under 25, present clauses 1, 2, and 5 only. They are close to
universal and none of them reads as defensive.
