# Scope creep taxonomy

Reference for the Scope Creep Auditor. Seventeen recognised patterns. Match findings to
a pattern by name so the output is consistent across audits.

Each entry: the pattern, the phrases that signal it, the mechanism by which it costs
money, and a typical exposure range for a small agency engagement. Exposure figures are
estimates for calibration, not promises.

---

## A. Unbounded quantity

### A1 — Undefined deliverable count
**Signals:** "some social assets", "a few variations", "assorted", "content for the
channels", "and the usual extras"
**Mechanism:** The client's mental number and yours differ by 3-10x. Nobody discovers
this until delivery, at which point refusing looks like bad faith.
**Exposure:** 8-40 hrs

### A2 — Unbounded revisions
**Signals:** "revisions as needed", "until we're happy", "a couple of rounds" (no
number), "minor tweaks along the way", "iterate together"
**Mechanism:** Revision cost is unbounded and the client has no incentive to converge.
Round four costs the same as round one and earns nothing.
**Exposure:** 10-60 hrs. The single most expensive pattern in agency work.

### A3 — Channel creep
**Signals:** "and we'll need it for LinkedIn too", "obviously it should work on all
platforms", "repurposed where relevant"
**Mechanism:** Each additional channel is a real reformat, re-crop, re-write — priced as
if it were a copy-paste.
**Exposure:** 2-6 hrs per channel

### A4 — Scope by adjective
**Signals:** "comprehensive", "full", "end-to-end", "holistic", "complete", "robust",
"in-depth"
**Mechanism:** Adjectives have no ceiling. "A comprehensive audit" is whatever the client
imagines when they read your invoice.
**Exposure:** highly variable; treat as a redefinition trigger, not an hours estimate

## B. Unbounded time

### B1 — Meeting load
**Signals:** "weekly syncs", "regular check-ins", "we'll stay close to this",
"collaborative process", "you'll join our standups"
**Mechanism:** A weekly hour-long call across a 12-week project is 12 hours plus prep and
context-switching, almost never in the quote.
**Exposure:** 15-30 hrs on a quarter-long engagement

### B2 — Post-delivery tail
**Signals:** "and support after launch", "ongoing tweaks", "we may need help bedding it
in", "hand-holding for the team"
**Mechanism:** No end date means no end. The project stays open, blocking capacity you
have already resold.
**Exposure:** 5-25 hrs, indefinite

### B3 — One-sided timeline
**Signals:** a fixed client deadline with no stated deadline for the client's own inputs,
approvals, or access
**Mechanism:** They deliver material late, the end date does not move, and the
compression comes out of your evenings.
**Exposure:** not hours — this converts every other risk into overtime

### B4 — Unpriced discovery
**Signals:** "we'd want you to get to know the business first", "immerse yourself",
"audit what we have"
**Mechanism:** Research is real work presented as courtesy. Often 2-3 days before
anything billable begins.
**Exposure:** 10-25 hrs

## C. Unbounded decisions

### C1 — No single approver
**Signals:** "the team will review", "we'll circulate internally", "once the wider group
has seen it", "stakeholders"
**Mechanism:** Every reviewer generates contradictory notes. You are not paid to
reconcile a client's internal disagreement, but you will.
**Exposure:** 6-20 hrs, and it degrades the work

### C2 — Late stakeholder
**Signals:** "our new CMO starts in March", "we'll want to run it past the board",
"pending leadership sign-off"
**Mechanism:** A stakeholder who joins after the decisions were made reopens the
decisions. Usually at the worst moment.
**Exposure:** 10-40 hrs of rework

### C3 — Undefined done
**Signals:** no acceptance criteria anywhere; "when it's right", "we'll know it when we
see it"
**Mechanism:** Without acceptance there is no completion, and without completion there is
no final invoice.
**Exposure:** unbounded — this is the pattern that produces unpaid invoices

## D. Unbounded obligation

### D1 — Assumed source material
**Signals:** silence on who writes the copy, sources the images, supplies data, gets
logins, or arranges interviews
**Mechanism:** Both sides assume the other is doing it. The one with the deadline blinks,
and that is you.
**Exposure:** 5-20 hrs

### D2 — Strategy smuggled into execution
**Signals:** an execution brief that expects positioning, messaging architecture, or
audience research as an unpriced input
**Mechanism:** You quoted for making the thing; they expected you to decide what the
thing should be.
**Exposure:** 15-40 hrs

### D3 — Rights and source files
**Signals:** silence on ownership; or "we'll own everything", "we'll need the working
files"
**Mechanism:** Source files, fonts, licensed stock, and reuse rights all carry real cost.
Assumed-free until it is awkward to charge.
**Exposure:** licence costs plus 2-8 hrs of packaging

### D4 — Reporting creep
**Signals:** "regular updates", "keep us posted", "a short monthly summary"
**Mechanism:** A "short summary" becomes a formatted deck once someone senior sees it.
**Exposure:** 2-5 hrs per cycle

### D5 — Competitive and benchmark requests
**Signals:** "how does this compare to [competitor]", "benchmark us against the market"
**Mechanism:** Research work arriving as a casual question inside an execution project.
**Exposure:** 4-12 hrs each

### D6 — The accumulation of small tweaks
**Signals:** no change-order threshold; "just a quick one", "while you're in there"
**Mechanism:** No individual request is worth invoicing for. Thirty of them are worth a
week.
**Exposure:** 10-30 hrs across a quarter

---

## Weighting note

When multiple patterns appear, C3 (undefined done) and A2 (unbounded revisions) dominate.
A brief with tight deliverable counts but no acceptance criteria is more dangerous than a
vague brief with a hard revision cap, because the first has no exit and the second has a
ceiling.
